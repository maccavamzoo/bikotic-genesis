# Giant Defy vs TCR vs Propel - Advanced Pro 0 versions: Which One Should You Buy?

Giant make three Advanced Pro 0 bikes at £5,299-£5,499. Same Ultegra Di2 groupset, same power meter. But three different use cases.

Here's the comparison to help you work out which one's for you.

![Giant Defy, TCR, and Propel Advanced Pro 0 comparison](/images/giant-pro-0-comparison-2026.jpg)

## What Giant Actually Say About Each Bike

### Defy Advanced Pro 0 - £5,499

![Giant Defy Advanced Pro 0](/images/giant-defy-advanced-pro-0-2026.jpg)

**Giant's Line:** "Here's to long rides. Climb higher mountains, skip the shortcuts. This buttery smooth endurance road bike helps you do more of what you love."

**Translation:** It's for people who actually ride their bikes. Long miles. All day. The sort of riding where comfort matters because you're still on the thing six hours later.

### TCR Advanced Pro 0 - £5,499

![Giant TCR Advanced Pro 0](/images/giant-tcr-advanced-pro-0-2026.jpg)

**Giant's Line:** "Superlight, ultra-efficient and rides like a dream. This well-rounded race bike blends industry-leading composite frame engineering with fully integrated components to help you climb, corner and descend with astonishing speed and sublime road handling."

**Translation:** It's Giant's all round light weight race bike. Does everything. Climbs, descends, sprints. If you could only have one bike, this is probably it.

### Propel Advanced Pro 0 - £5,299

![Giant Propel Advanced Pro 0](/images/giant-propel-advanced-pro-0-2026.jpg)

**Giant's Line:** "This aero rocketship is built for speed when it matters most. It's lighter than the previous generation and engineered to slice through wind. The result? Race-winning efficiency that puts you ahead of the pack."

**Translation:** It's for going fast on flatter roads. Bunch racing, sprinting, crits. Giant mention you could use it for time trials, and yeah you could, but this isn't a dedicated TT bike - it's an aero race bike for holding speed in bunches and launching sprints.

## The Key Differences

### Weight

Giant don't publish actual weights for any of these bikes. The TCR is the lightest, the Propel's in the middle, the Defy's the heaviest because of the endurance features. How much difference? No idea. Giant won't tell you.

### Aerodynamics (All 3 bikes have integrated brake hoses)

**Propel wins.** It's got AeroSystem Shaping with truncated ellipse tubes and a full aero cockpit. Built for cutting through wind.

**TCR** has optimized tube shaping - Giant claim 4.19 watts better than the old model.

**Defy** no aero claims, other than integrated hoses.

### Comfort

**Defy wins.** D-Fuse seatpost and handlebar specifically engineered to absorb road shocks. Plus relaxed geometry and 40mm tire clearance.

**TCR and Propel** are race bikes. Stiff and aggressive. Fine for racers. Maybe not ideal for old blokes on 6-hour rides.

### Tire Clearance

- **Defy:** 40mm max - fit proper big tires, fender mounts, makes a good winter training bike
- **TCR:** 33mm max - sensible range
- **Propel:** 32mm max - aero-focused

### Geometry (Size ML)

| Measurement | Defy | TCR | Propel |
|------------|------|-----|--------|
| **Stack** | 577mm | 562mm | 562mm |
| **Reach** | 384mm | 393mm | 393mm |
| **Chainstays** | 420mm | 405mm | 405mm |

Defy: more upright, less stretched, longer rear end = stable and comfortable.

TCR/Propel: lower, longer, shorter rear = aggressive race geometry.

### Gearing

- **Defy:** 50/34 with 11-34 cassette - compact for climbing
- **TCR:** 52/36 with 11-34 cassette - semi-compact all-rounder
- **Propel:** 52/36 with 11-30 cassette - tighter ratios for flat speed

## What They All Share

**Shimano Ultegra Di2** - 12-speed electronic. Reliable, perfect shifting.

**Giant Power Pro Power Meter** - Integrated cranks. Saves you £500-800 vs aftermarket.

**Giant SLR Carbon Wheels** - Defy/TCR get SLR 0 40mm depth, Propel gets SLR 1 50mm.

**CADEX Tubeless Tires** - Defy: 32mm, TCR: 30mm, Propel: 28mm.

---

## The Verdict

**Get the Defy** if comfort and all-day riding matters more than pure speed. The D-Fuse tech works, 40mm clearance gives options, geometry won't cripple you. Best for sportives, gran fondos, Fred Whitton Challenge, Dragon Ride, winter training.

**Get the TCR** if you want the best all-rounder. Lightest, climbs brilliantly, still got decent aero, versatile geometry. The "if I could only have one bike" answer. Best for road racing, hilly events, Alpine trips, all-round fast riding.

**Get the Propel** if you're racing flats and want maximum aero. Real gains on flat roads, £200 cheaper, focused tool for speed. Not really an all-rounder. Best for flat crits, bunch racing, fast chain gangs.

**Important:** The geometry is identical across each range. A £3,199 Defy has the same stack/reach as this £5,499 one. Pick the bike type for your riding first, then pick your budget.
