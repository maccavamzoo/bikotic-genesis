# Specialized Tarmac SL8 Pro vs Trek Madone SLR 7: Which One Should You Buy?

Specialized say the Tarmac SL8 is "the world's fastest race bike". Trek say the Madone Gen 8 merged their lightest and fastest bikes into one. Both £7,249-7,250 with Ultegra Di2. Both claim to be the best.

Here's the comparison with actual specs, not marketing baloney.

![Specialized Tarmac SL8 vs Trek Madone SLR 7 comparison](/images/tarmac-sl8-madone-comparison.jpg)

---

## What The Brands Actually Say About Each Bike

### Specialized Tarmac SL8 Pro - £7,249

![Specialized Tarmac SL8 Pro](/images/specialized-tarmac-sl8-pro-2025.jpg)

**Specialized's Line:** "Nothing is faster than the Tarmac SL8 thanks to a combination of aerodynamics, lightweight, and ride quality previously thought impossible. The world's fastest race bike."

**What That Actually Means:** They made it more aero than the Venge (their old dedicated aero bike), 15% lighter than the SL7, and claim 16.6 seconds saved over 40km. The 685g frame is genuinely light. They've optimized the shape at the front (Speed Sniffer nose cone) and narrowed the seat post. Comes with a 4iiii power meter included.

### Trek Madone SLR 7 Gen 8 - £7,250

![Trek Madone SLR 7 Gen 8](/images/trek-madone-slr-7-gen8-2025.jpg)

**Trek's Line:** "The Madone SLR 7 Gen 8 is an aero, featherlight road bike that's built to race. Its 900 Series OCLV Carbon frame keeps the weight way down while delivering aerodynamic gains and IsoFlow technology means you don't have to sacrifice comfort for speed."

**What That Actually Means:** Trek killed the Emonda (their lightweight bike) and merged it with the Madone. This Gen 8 is their lightest Madone Disc frameset ever and as light as the old Emonda frameset. Uses 900 Series OCLV carbon (20% stronger than previous 800 Series), has the IsoFlow cutout for compliance, and Trek claim it beats both the old Madone Gen 7 AND the old Emonda on any gradient. No power meter included.

## The Key Differences

### Weight

**Specialized Tarmac SL8 Pro:** 
- Frame: 685g (claimed)
- Complete bike: 7.25kg (size 56)

**Trek Madone SLR 7 Gen 8:**
- Frame + fork: 1,146g combined
- Complete bike: 7.52kg (ML size, with tubeless sealant)

The Tarmac is 270g lighter as a complete bike. 

### Aerodynamics

**Specialized:** More aero than the Venge. Speed Sniffer nose cone up front, narrow seat post out back. They claim 16.6 seconds saved over 40km vs the SL7, 128 seconds over Milan-San Remo.

**Trek:** Revolutionary Full System Foil tube shapes. IsoFlow cutout is claimed to be both aero and comfortable (because it lets air flow through). They tested against the old Madone Gen 7 and Emonda to make sure this Gen 8 beats both.

Both brands tested against their own previous bikes in wind tunnels. Neither publish proper head-to-head comparisons. Both claim to be fastest. Take it with a pinch of salt.

### Comfort & Compliance

**Specialized:** 33% better stiffness-to-weight vs SL7, but 6% more compliant in the saddle. That compliance is built into the carbon layup. 32mm tire clearance.

**Trek:** IsoFlow technology - that distinctive cutout at the top of the seat tube. Trek claim 80% more compliance than without it. The hole flexes, simple as that. 32mm tire clearance.

Both have made comfort a priority despite being race bikes. The Madone's approach is more visible (you can literally see the cutout), the Tarmac's is engineered into the frame construction.

### Geometry (Size 56 / ML Comparison)

| Measurement | Tarmac SL8 (56) | Madone SLR 7 (ML) |
|------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Stack** | 565mm | 565.7mm |
| **Reach** | 395mm | 389mm |
| **Chainstays** | 410mm | 410mm |
| **Head Tube Angle** | 73.5° | 73.5° |
| **Seat Tube Angle** | 73.5° | 73.4° |
| **Wheelbase** | 991mm | 980mm |

Nearly identical geometry. The Madone is 6mm shorter in reach, which means slightly less stretched out. Both aggressive race positions.

### Gearing & Wheels

**Specialized Tarmac SL8 Pro:**
- Shimano Ultegra R8100 Di2 with 4iiii Precision 3+ power meter
- 52/36 with 11-30 cassette
- Roval Rapide CL III wheels: 51mm front, 48.5mm rear (asymmetric)
- 28mm S-Works Turbo TLR tires

**Trek Madone SLR 7 Gen 8:**
- Shimano Ultegra R8100 Di2 (no power meter)
- 52/36 with 11-30 cassette  
- Bontrager Aeolus Pro 51 wheels: 51mm front and rear (symmetric)
- 28mm Bontrager R3 Hard-Case Lite TLR tires

Same gearing, same groupset. Specialized give you the power meter (£500-600 value), Trek don't. Specialized use asymmetric wheel depths (they claim it's faster - shallower rear saves weight), Trek go 51mm all round.

### Cockpit

**Specialized:** Roval Rapide integrated bar/stem. Fully integrated cables, aero optimized. Proprietary.

**Trek:** Trek Aero RSL integrated bar/stem. Same deal, fully integrated, aero-focused. Also proprietary.

There is no option to alter the stem length or bar width at the time of purchase. Both include RSL Aero bottles and cages (Trek) or similar aero bottle systems.

## What They Both Share

**Shimano Ultegra Di2 R8100** - Same 12-speed electronic shifting. Reliable, perfect shifting every time.

**Integrated Everything** - Both bikes have fully integrated cables, aero cockpits, internal routing for everything. Clean, fast, bit of a pain to service.

**32mm Tire Clearance** - Enough for proper road tires, not quite gravel territory. Both come with 28mm fitted.

**Aggressive Race Geometry** - Neither is an endurance bike. Both are aggressive race positions.

**T47 Threaded Bottom Brackets** - Easier to service than press-fit.

**Hydraulic Disc Brakes** - Shimano Ultegra R8170 on both. 160mm rotors. Plenty of stopping power.

## How Do They Ride?

### Specialized Tarmac SL8

The 685g frame weight is real. You feel it on climbs - the bike accelerates like it's angry. That 33% stiffness-to-weight improvement shows up when you're out of the saddle hammering. But that 6% compliance increase stops it feeling harsh - Specialized actually delivered on the "stiff and smooth" claim.

The Speed Sniffer nose cone looks daft but the aero claims seem legitimate. On longer rides you notice it feels efficient - you're working less hard to maintain speed. The Rapide asymmetric wheels (51/48.5mm) are noticeably quicker than the old SL7 wheels.

The 4iiii power meter just works. Left-crank only, but it's accurate and saves you £500-600 vs adding aftermarket.

**Sizing note:** Runs true to size. Specialized's geometry is proven and hasn't changed much from SL7.

### Trek Madone SLR 7 Gen 8

The IsoFlow cutout is not marketing nonsense - the rear end genuinely flexes more without feeling flexy when you're sprinting. On rough roads you notice the difference. It's comfortable for a race bike.

The 7.52kg complete weight is competitive. Multiple owners report 1-2mph faster average speeds on the same routes vs the old Emonda, which backs up Trek's claim that this thing's properly fast.

The 900 Series OCLV carbon feels stiffer than the old 800 Series. The bike doesn't waste watts. The Full System Foil shaping works - it holds speed on flats and rollers.

## The Verdict

**Get the Tarmac SL8 Pro** if you're obsessed with frame weight and want the power meter included. Specialized have gone all-in on making this the lightest aero bike possible, and the 685g frame backs that up. The asymmetric Rapide wheels make sense (lighter rear, more aero front), and you get that 4iiii power meter. Essentially £750+ value over the Madone when you account for the power meter. Best for climbers who still want aero, all-round racers, riders who value that power meter inclusion and trust Specialized's data-driven approach. It's a bit odd looking at the best of times, and if you're not going to slam it the spacers can look a bit of a dog's dinner, and who knows what that does to the aero claims....

**Get the Madone SLR 7** if you want Trek's refined approach and IsoFlow compliance actually matters to you. It's 270g heavier complete, but that IsoFlow cutout provides real compliance. The 900 Series OCLV carbon is 20% stronger than the old 800 Series. No power meter, but you're only £1 more expensive than the Tarmac - add your own if you want one. Best for all-round racers, riders who want proven IsoFlow compliance, people who value Trek's refinement and customization options. Again, it's a weird looking bike, you decide if it's your cup of tea!

**Real Talk:** Both bikes are within 300g of each other fully built. Both have nearly identical geometry. Both claim to be the fastest (spoiler: they're both fast). The differences are marginal enough that you should test ride both and buy whichever one feels better to *you*. If you can find somewhere to test ride them that is.

The Tarmac gives you more for your money if you value that power meter. The Madone gives you Trek's proven IsoFlow tech and 900 Series carbon if you value their engineering approach.

Your choice: Specialized's "sniffer nose thing" approach, or Trek's "funny little hole" philosophy. Both are fast bikes. Neither is a bad choice.
